The May 9 target is a blue U on a very pale blue background, with a green disk behind the top of each branch. The tops of the branches are rounded. fmata draws it with two rules. One radial gradient on html paints the two green disks, a small blue disk in each, and the pale background. A border on the body, without its top, draws the U, and the small blue disks round off its branches.

**Wide view** shows the copies of the background tile around the canvas. **Teaching colors** give the gradient's disk and the body's border different colors: in the real colors, both are the same blue.

## The code, spaced out

```css
<style>
& {                          /* at the top level: html */
  background:
    radial-gradient(1Q,      /* a circle of radius 1Q = 0.945px */
      #08C 4MM,              /* blue up to 4mm = 15.12px */
      #A6CE39 0 37q,         /* green from 15.12 (the 0 is raised) to 37q = 34.96px */
      #0991)                 /* the last color, raised to 37q, fills the rest */
    13% -32q /               /* position: the tile at (29.89, −30.24) */
    45MM;                    /* size: 170.08 wide, auto height = 300 */
  * {                        /* nested: every descendant of html; only body is displayed */
    margin: 30% 25% 70;      /* 120 at the top, 100 on the sides, 70px at the bottom */
    border: 32q solid #08C;  /* 32q = 30.24px, drawn 30px wide */
    border-top: 0            /* no top: a U; the end of the code closes both { */
```

## How it works

### 1. One background on html

At the top level of a style sheet, `&` means the root of the document: html. My Chrome gives the same picture with `html{`.

The background of the root element is painted over the whole canvas. Its positioning area is html's box. The code has no doctype, so the page is in quirks mode, and there html fills the window: my Chrome measures it at (0, 0), 400 × 300.

### 2. A 170.08 × 300 tile

After the `/`, `45MM` is the size of the background image. Units ignore case: `MM` is `mm`. 1in = 96px = 25.4mm, so 45mm = 170.08px.

There is only one value: the height is `auto`. A gradient has no size of its own, so an `auto` height takes the height of the positioning area, html's box: 300. With `/45MM 100%`, the picture is the same. The tile is 170.08 × 300.

### 3. A circle of radius 1Q

`radial-gradient(1Q,…)` starts with a single length: the shape is a circle of that radius, at the center of the tile, (85.04, 150). `Q` is the quarter-millimeter: 1in = 101.6Q, so 1Q = 0.945px.

The color stops are lengths, measured from the center. They can go past the radius:

- `#08C 4MM`: blue from the center up to 4mm = 15.12px.
- `#A6CE39 0 37q`: a position smaller than an earlier one is raised to the largest earlier one. The `0` becomes 15.12. Green runs from 15.12 to 37q = 34.96px, with a sharp edge on both sides.
- `#0991` has no position. The last stop sits at 100% of the gradient's radius by default, and that radius is 1Q, 0.945px. This is smaller than 37q, so it is raised to 34.96px too. The change from green to `#0991` is sharp, and the last color fills the rest of the tile.

That is why the radius is 1Q. With `circle` in place of `1Q` (136 characters), the circle gets its default size, and the last stop sits at the farthest corner of the tile, 172.4px from the center: the green fades into the background over the whole tile, and 103,214 pixels change by more than 8 levels compared with my render of the official code.

### 4. #0991 on white

`#0991` is a 4-digit hex color with alpha: `#00999911`. The alpha is 0x11 = 17/255 = 0.067. My Chrome computes it as `rgba(0, 153, 153, 0.067)`.

The canvas under html is white. A blue-green at 6.7% over white is a very pale blue: my render shows (238, 248, 248), `#eef8f8`, everywhere outside the disks.

### 5. The tile at (29.89, −30.24)

`13%-32q` is the position. A percentage lines up the point at 13% of the tile with the point at 13% of the area. Horizontally, that is 13% × (400 − 170.08) = 29.89.

Vertically, the tile is exactly as tall as the area, so a percentage would not move it. A length does: −32q = −30.24px. With no vertical value (`13%`, 128 characters), the disks sit at y = 150, and 7,088 pixels change.

So the circle's center is at (29.89 + 85.04, −30.24 + 150) = (114.93, 119.76). With `29.89px -30.24px/170.08px` in place of `13%-32q/45MM`, my render is the same.

### 6. The tile repeats

A background repeats by default: a copy every 170.08px across and every 300px down. Across, the next copy's center is at 114.93 + 170.08 = 285.01: that is the second disk. The copies at x = −55.15 and 455.09 fall off the canvas. Down, the copies' centers are at y = −180.24 and 419.76, off the canvas too. On the canvas, there are exactly two disks, and `#0991` everywhere else.

With `no-repeat` after `45MM` (142 characters), there is only one tile: one green disk, on a pale rectangle from x = 29.89 to 199.97 and down to y = 269.76, with white around it. The right branch ends square. 69,012 pixels change.

### 7. The body's box

`*{…}` is nested in `&{…}`: it means `& *`, every descendant of html. head and style are not displayed, which leaves the body.

`margin:30%25%70` gives the top, the left and right, then the bottom. Percentage margins are a share of the containing block's width, 400: 30% = 120 at the top, 25% = 100 on the sides. In quirks mode, `70` with no unit means 70px.

In quirks mode, the body also stretches to fill html's height, minus its margins: 300 − 120 − 70 = 110. My Chrome measures the body at (100, 120), 200 × 110.

### 8. A border without the top

`border:32q solid#08C`: 32q = 30.24px. My Chrome computes the border width as 30px: it rounds border widths to whole pixels. With `border:30px`, the picture is the same.

`border-top:0` removes the top. What is left is a U: two branches 30 wide, from x = 100 to 130 and from 270 to 300, joined by the bottom border from y = 200 to 230. Without `border-top:0` (119 characters), the box is closed at the top and 4,200 pixels change.

The branches end flat at y = 120. The rounded tops come from the gradient: each blue disk, 30.24 wide, is centered 0.24px above the end of a branch, at x = 114.93 and 285.01, almost on the branch's middle line (115 and 285). The body's border is painted over html's background, so it hides the bottom half of the blue disk and the green ring under each branch. The top half of the blue disk stays visible and rounds the branch. Without the blue disk (`radial-gradient(1Q,#A6CE39 37q,#0991)`, 121 characters), the branches end square inside the green disks, and 820 pixels change.

## Golf notes

Below, a pixel counts as changed when one of its channels moves by more than 8, compared with my render of the official code.

- `&{` reaches html in 2 characters. With `html{` (135 characters), the picture is the same.
- `*{`, nested in `&{`, reaches the body in 2 characters: head and style are not displayed. With `body{` (135 characters), the picture is the same.
- `1Q` makes the gradient a circle in 2 characters, and its tiny radius pulls the last stop down to 37q with no position. With `radial-gradient(circle,` and `#0991 0)` (138 characters), the picture is the same.
- One gradient paints three things: the blue disks, the green rings and the background. The background color is the gradient's last color, so it needs no `background-color`.
- `4MM` writes 15.12px in 3 characters. With `15px` (133 characters), 16 pixels change.
- `#A6CE39 0` starts the green where the blue ends: the `0` is raised. With `#A6CE39 4MM 37q` (134 characters), the picture is the same.
- `37q` writes 34.96px in 3 characters. With `35px` (133 characters), 112 pixels change.
- `#0991` gives the pale background in 5 characters, through its alpha. With the opaque `#eef8f8` (134 characters), the picture is the same: no channel moves by more than 1.
- The position and size follow the gradient with no space: `)13%-32q/45MM`. With `) 13% -32q/45MM` (134 characters), the picture is the same.
- `13%` writes 29.89px in 3 characters. With `30px` (133 characters), 336 pixels change.
- `-32q` writes −30.24px in 4 characters. With `-30px` (133 characters), 480 pixels change.
- `45MM` writes 170.08px in 4 characters, and the height is free: `auto` is 300, the canvas height. With `170px` (133 characters), the second disk moves 0.11px to the left, and 609 pixels change.
- `30%25%70` writes 120, 100 and 70px with no spaces: a space is not needed after `%`, and a unitless 70 is 70px in quirks mode. With `120px 100px 70px` (140 characters), the picture is the same.
- `32q` writes the 30px border in 3 characters. With `30px` (133 characters), the picture is the same.
- `solid#08C` needs no space before `#`. With `solid #08C` (133 characters), the picture is the same.
- The repeat is free: it is the default, and it gives the second disk.
- The code ends inside the rules: the end of the code closes them. With `}}` at the end (134 characters), the picture is the same.
